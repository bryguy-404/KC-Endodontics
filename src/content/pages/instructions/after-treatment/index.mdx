## What happens after root canal treatment is completed?

The root canal system has been cleaned, filled, and sealed, but the access opening in the crown of the tooth may still have a temporary restoration. Contact your restorative dentist promptly for the permanent filling, crown, or other restoration recommended to protect the tooth from fracture and reinfection.

KC Endodontics sends a treatment report to the restorative dentist and may recommend a follow-up examination or imaging to monitor healing.

## How should I protect the tooth before it is restored?

Avoid heavy chewing and hard foods on the treated tooth until the permanent restoration is complete. Continue gentle brushing and flossing. If the temporary material comes out, the bite feels significantly uneven, or the tooth fractures, call the office.

## What symptoms deserve a call?

Contact KC Endodontics if severe pain or pressure lasts more than a few days, swelling appears or increases, a medication reaction develops, altered sensation is present, the temporary restoration is lost, or the symptoms that led to treatment return.

## Are there additional considerations after endodontic surgery?

Depending on the tooth and surgical area, the nearby anatomy can include sensory nerves or the sinus. Dr. Moore evaluates these relationships before treatment and provides procedure-specific instructions when they are relevant.

After surgery on an upper tooth near the sinus, you may be asked to avoid creating pressure in the sinus area and to follow specific sneezing or nose-blowing instructions. After lower-tooth surgery near a sensory nerve, report persistent tingling, numbness, or altered sensation. Postoperative infection is uncommon but requires evaluation when increasing swelling, drainage, fever, or worsening pain develops.

## Does the tooth still need routine dental care?

Yes. A treated tooth still depends on healthy gums, supporting bone, a sound restoration, and daily hygiene. Continue regular dental examinations and contact either office if the tooth develops new symptoms in the future.

> These are general expectations. The written instructions for your tooth and procedure take priority.
