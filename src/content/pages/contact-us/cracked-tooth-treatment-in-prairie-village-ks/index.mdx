## Warning signs of a possible crack

A cracked tooth may cause a sharp pain while chewing, discomfort when you release biting pressure, sensitivity to hot or cold, or pain that appears and disappears without an obvious pattern. Sometimes the tooth becomes sore on its own as the pulp grows more inflamed.

Because the crack can open and close with pressure, symptoms may be difficult to reproduce. Small cracks are not always visible on a standard dental X-ray, so diagnosis may involve a detailed history, bite testing, magnification, light, periodontal evaluation, and other focused tests.

## Can the tooth be saved?

That depends on the type, location, and depth of the crack. Superficial craze lines and some fractured cusps may need little or no endodontic treatment. A crack that reaches the pulp may be treated with root canal therapy and a protective crown if enough sound tooth remains.

A split tooth generally cannot be saved intact, and a crack that extends too far below the gumline may make the tooth non-restorable. A vertical root fracture may require extraction or, in selected cases, surgery to remove the fractured portion.

Early evaluation matters because cracks do not heal and can continue to spread.

## Common questions

### Does every cracked tooth need a root canal?

No. Treatment depends on whether the crack has affected the pulp, how far it extends, and whether the tooth can be restored. Some fractured cusps can be repaired by a restorative dentist without endodontic treatment.

### Will a crown stop a crack from getting worse?

A crown can provide important protection, but no treatment can guarantee that a crack will never progress. The evaluation helps determine whether a crown, root canal treatment, surgery, extraction, or monitoring is appropriate.

### Should I wait if the pain comes and goes?

Intermittent pain is common with cracks and does not mean the problem has resolved. Earlier diagnosis generally offers more options than waiting for a crack to spread or the pulp to become infected.

### What if the tooth broke because of an injury?

Call promptly. An impact can injure the pulp and supporting tissues even if the visible damage appears small, and neighboring teeth may also need to be checked.
