## Brief sensitivity to hot or cold

Sensitivity that lasts only a moment may be related to minor decay, a loose filling, gum recession, or an exposed root surface. Use a soft toothbrush and arrange a dental evaluation if the sensitivity persists, worsens, or becomes spontaneous.

## Sensitivity after recent dental work

Dental treatment can temporarily irritate the pulp. Symptoms should gradually improve. If sensitivity lasts for several weeks, becomes stronger, or begins lingering after the temperature is gone, contact your dentist or endodontist.

## Sharp pain while biting

Pain with chewing or when biting pressure is released can come from decay, a loose restoration, a crack, or damage to the pulp. Because cracked-tooth symptoms often come and go, focused testing may be needed to locate the source.

## Lingering hot or cold pain

Pain that continues after a hot or cold stimulus is removed can suggest that the pulp has been significantly damaged by decay, a crack, or trauma. Arrange an evaluation promptly; earlier diagnosis can provide more options for preserving the tooth.

## Constant pressure, tenderness, or swelling

Severe pain, pressure, gum swelling, a pimple-like bump, or tenderness to touch may indicate an abscess or infection involving the surrounding tissues. Call KC Endodontics promptly. If swelling is rapidly increasing or you have difficulty breathing or swallowing, seek emergency medical care.

## A dull ache in the upper teeth and jaw

Sinus pressure and tooth grinding can both create discomfort that feels as if it is coming from the upper teeth. A dental or medical evaluation may be necessary when the cause is uncertain, severe, or persistent.

## Pain felt in the head, neck, or ear

An injured tooth can refer pain to other areas, but not every facial or head pain begins in a tooth. An endodontic evaluation can help determine whether the source is dental and identify when another dental or medical specialist should be involved.

> This guide can help describe symptoms, but it cannot identify the tooth or confirm the diagnosis. Call when pain is ongoing or changing.
