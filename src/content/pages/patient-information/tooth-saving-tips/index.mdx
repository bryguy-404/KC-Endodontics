## Why keeping a natural tooth matters

A restorable natural tooth maintains normal bite relationships, efficient chewing, and a familiar appearance. Preserving it may also avoid the additional procedures needed to replace a missing tooth with an implant, bridge, or removable appliance.

## Questions to ask before extraction

1. Can this tooth be predictably saved with root canal treatment and a final restoration?
2. Is a specialist evaluation appropriate before I make the decision?
3. If the tooth is removed, how will it be replaced and what additional treatment will that require?
4. What are the realistic benefits, limitations, costs, and long-term outlook of each option?

## When extraction may still be appropriate

Saving a tooth is not always possible or advisable. A deep fracture, insufficient remaining structure, severe periodontal support loss, non-restorable decay, or an unfavorable long-term outlook may make extraction the more predictable option.

## Avoid decisions based only on old assumptions

Modern anesthesia and endodontic techniques are intended to relieve pain, not create it. Root canal treatment is often completed in one or two endodontic visits, followed by restorative care. Extraction and replacement can require several additional steps.

> The right decision depends on the individual tooth—not a rule that every tooth must be saved or every painful tooth must be removed.
