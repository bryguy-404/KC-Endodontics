## Evaluate the whole tooth and the whole patient

Treatment planning for a compromised tooth may involve pulpal and periapical diagnosis, remaining tooth structure, periodontal support, restorability, previous treatment quality, anatomy, symptoms, medical considerations, patient preferences, and the likely long-term restorative plan.

The decision should be individualized. No single image, symptom, or rule determines the right option for every patient.

## AAE Treatment Options Guide

The American Association of Endodontists’ *Treatment Options for the Compromised Tooth: A Decision Guide* presents case examples with radiographs and clinical photographs, clinical considerations, and prognosis-based guidance.

[Open the AAE Treatment Options Guide](https://www.aae.org/specialty/clinical-resources/treatment-planning/treatment-options-guide/)

The guide can support conversations about:

- Initial nonsurgical root canal treatment
- Nonsurgical retreatment
- Endodontic surgery
- Cracks, fractures, resorption, and procedural complications
- Periodontal and restorative considerations
- When extraction may be necessary
